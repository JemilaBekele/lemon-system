Yes, this can be done: keep two separate systems with two databases, and connect them with a small, controlled link.

**The three rules**

1. **The stock system is the only place products are created.** It's where purchases, batches and stock live, so it should own the product list.
2. **The e-commerce system receives products from stock and adds only online-shop details:** brand, photos, features, directions, discounts, featured/hot-selling. Its staff never type a product name.
3. **An e-commerce order becomes a sale in stock when it's confirmed.** The company keeps creating its own sales directly in stock exactly as today. Both kinds appear in the same sales list, labelled by source.

Your two schemas already make this easy. Both have `Product` and `SubProduct` with a unique `productCode` / `subProductCode`, the same Category → SubCategory → UnitOfMeasure layout, and the same `Customer` idea. The e-commerce schema even has an unused `SyncLog` table you can use.

## 1. One product list (stock to e-commerce)

**Who owns which field**

| Owned by stock (read-only in e-commerce) | Owned by e-commerce only |
|---|---|
| productCode, name, generic, category, subcategory, unit, sub-products, isActive, base price | brand, images, features, specs, packaging, directions, warranty, discounts, featured / hot-selling / new arrival, "show online" switch |

**Schema changes**
- **E-commerce `Product` and `SubProduct`:** add `stockProductId` / `stockSubProductId` (unique) and `isPublished` (default false). Do the same for Category, SubCategory and UnitOfMeasure, so they come from stock too.
- **Stock:** add an outbox table, e.g. `IntegrationOutbox { id, type, payload, status, attempts, lastError }`.

**How it flows**
1. Staff create or edit a product in stock. In the same transaction, stock writes a row to the outbox.
2. A job in stock (you already have `node-cron`) sends pending rows to an internal e-commerce endpoint, e.g. `POST /api/v1/integration/products/upsert`. That endpoint creates or updates the product by `stockProductId`.
3. If e-commerce is down, the row stays pending and is retried. Saving a product in stock never fails because of the online shop.
4. In the e-commerce dashboard, "Create product" becomes **"Products from stock"**. Staff choose which products to show online and add photos, brand and descriptions.
5. A **"Sync all"** button runs a full resync and records it in `SyncLog`. Use it once at the start to match existing e-commerce products by `productCode`, and afterwards whenever something gets out of step.

## 2. E-commerce orders become stock sales

**Schema changes in stock**
- **`Sell`:** add `source` (enum `STOCK` | `ECOMMERCE`, default `STOCK`), `externalOrderId` (unique) and `externalOrderNumber`.
- **`Customer`:** add `externalCustomerId` (unique).
- **Which shop fills online orders:** a `SellItem` needs a `shopId`. Add `stockShopId` to e-commerce `WarehouseLocation`, or set one "Online Shop" in stock settings.

**How it flows**
1. Staff confirm an order in e-commerce (status `CONFIRMED`). E-commerce writes an outbox row and sends it to stock: `POST /api/integration/ecommerce-orders`. The payload includes the order number, customer (name, phone, TIN, company), and items (`stockProductId`, `stockSubProductId`, quantity, unit price after discount), plus delivery fee and totals.
2. Stock:
   - creates or updates the customer by `externalCustomerId` (falling back to phone)
   - creates a `Sell` with `source = ECOMMERCE` and `saleStatus = NOT_APPROVED`, priced as the customer paid
3. Store staff handle it like any other sale: choose batches, approve, deliver. **Stock only goes down at your existing approval step**, so batch, FIFO and ledger logic stay in one place.
4. Because `externalOrderId` is unique, a repeated message can never create a second sale.
5. If e-commerce cancels the order before delivery, it sends a cancel message and stock cancels that `Sell`.

**The company's own orders:** nothing changes. Staff keep creating sales in stock, with `source = STOCK`. Add a "Source" filter and badge to the sales list so online and in-house sales can be told apart.

## 3. Sending information back (phase 2, optional)
- **Order status:** when stock marks a sale **Delivered** or **Cancelled**, it tells e-commerce to update the order, and the customer gets the existing notification.
- **Availability:** stock sends "in stock / out of stock" per product, so the shop can hide products or show "out of stock". This works well with the new product-level warning quantity.

## Security
- **Not public:** the integration endpoints are separate from the user-facing routes and don't use user logins.
- **Shared key:** each side checks a shared secret header, e.g. `X-Integration-Key`, set in both `.env` files. An HMAC signature is better still.

## Decisions the owner needs to make
1. **Price:** does the stock `sellPrice` become the online base price, or does e-commerce set its own? (Tier discounts stay in e-commerce either way.)
2. **Fulfilment:** which shop or store fills online orders? One online shop, or one per warehouse?
3. **Timing:** should the stock sale be created when the order is **confirmed** (my suggestion) or when it's placed?
4. **Delivery fee:** should it go on the stock sale as a line or note?

## Order to build it
1. Product link: schema fields, stock outbox, e-commerce upsert endpoint, "Sync all", and the e-commerce "Products from stock" screen.
2. Orders into stock: `source` and `externalOrderId`, the stock endpoint, and the confirm hook in e-commerce.
3. Status and availability going back.
